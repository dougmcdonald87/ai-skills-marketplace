## The pitch, and why it changed

The first version of these drafts said *"you set the price, and it goes to your own PayPal.me"*.
**That was wrong**, and the creator research is why.

Nearly every creator's skills are MIT-licensed and free, and there is already a large free install
directory (`skills.sh`) with install counts in the hundreds of thousands. Asking someone to list
their free MIT skill at £19 does not work — the licence permits it, but the buyer would never pay
for a download they can get for nothing.

So each message now makes **two separate offers**, and the creator picks:

1. **List your skills free.** A verified, curated entry that links to your repo. Costs them
   nothing, and the entry says what the skill does, what it needs, and who wrote it.
2. **If you have something that is genuinely not free** — a bundle, a supported edition, a tier,
   a paid course — the register has a checkout for it, takes no commission, and the money goes
   straight to your PayPal link.

That second offer is the actual business, and it is the one thing the research found genuinely
under-served: almost none of these creators have any way to be paid for anything at all.

Full reasoning, with the evidence, is in `docs/strategy/findings-2026-10-18.md`.
