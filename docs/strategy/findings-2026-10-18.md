# Market findings and positioning — 18 October 2026

This is the honest read of the creator research in `.scratch/creator-outreach-list.md`, and it is
**not good news for the original thesis**. It is written down so the decision is made on evidence
rather than on hope.

## What the research found

### 1. The skills distribution market is far more mature than the original REA scan suggested

The original research found agent memory/persistence and MCP marketplaces to be the gaps, and
skills-adjacent tooling to be saturated. The creator research shows the skills side specifically is
**well served**:

- `skills.sh` is a large public install directory with a leaderboard of install counts. It states
  plainly on its own site: *"Skills are open source on GitHub."* No listing fee, no revenue share.
- `npx skills add <owner>/<repo>` is a cross-harness install CLI behind it.
- Anthropic's own plugin marketplace at `claude.com/plugins` is first-party distribution.
- `aaskills.tech` already runs a skills catalogue with 2,600+ entries.
- `obra/superpowers-marketplace` and `wshobson/agents` (94 plugins) are existing marketplaces.

**Implication:** the register is not entering an empty field. It is a new storefront in a street
that already has footfall.

### 2. "Platform-agnostic" is not a moat — it is table stakes

The brief locked the moat as *cross-platform neutrality*. The research found it claimed by almost
every credible creator:

- Addy Osmani — *"Works with 70+ agents"*
- Alireza Rezvani — 13 AI coding tools, 388 skills
- Ahmad Othman Adi — *"Codex, Cursor, OpenCode, 60+ agents"*
- Steph Ango — *"follow the Agent Skills specification… any skills-compatible agent"*
- Seth Hobson — 7 named harnesses
- Diego Valbuena and others, replicating one skill across every per-harness directory

**Implication:** a buyer who wants platform-agnostic already has `skills.sh` and a dozen
free catalogues. Claiming it does not differentiate.

### 3. The skills are almost all free and permissively licensed

Of 24 verified creators, 22 are MIT or Apache-2.0. Two are non-commercial (CC BY-NC) and cannot be
resold without relicensing. The whole point of a permissive licence is that anyone may use, copy and
sell it.

**Implication:** *why would anyone pay £29 for a skill they can download free?* The register's
original model — buyer pays a creator, creator keeps the money — has a hole in it that the licence
terms themselves create.

### 4. The real, verified gap: creators have no way to charge

This is the one thing the research found that is genuinely under-served, and it is consistent
across the whole list:

- Most creators publish free skills and have **no monetisation path at all** — no PayPal.me, no
  checkout, no listing, no way to take money for anything.
- The ones who *do* charge have built their own infrastructure: `ecc.tools` ($19/active seat/month),
  Primer Radiant, Product Compass, cfo.ai sponsorship, a private CSO evaluation.
- Nobody charges a **one-off price for a single skill**. That is precisely the gap the register sits
  in, and it is the one thing nobody has built.

## What this means

**The register's value to a creator is not distribution.** `skills.sh` has install counts in the
hundreds of thousands; a new site with four entries has none.

**The register's value to a creator is a checkout.** They have the skill, the skill is free, and
they have no way to be paid for it. The register gives them a PayPal link, a number, a verified
listing and someone who has actually run their skill.

**The register's value to a buyer is not access.** Access is free. The value is curation — a
catalogue where every entry has been read and run by a person, with the price and the publisher
stated — and a single purchase instead of hunting through install counts.

## Three viable models, honestly assessed

| Model | What it is | Honest assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Curated paid listing** (current) | Creator lists a skill, buyer pays once, money goes to the creator's PayPal | Works only if the listed thing is *not* otherwise free — i.e. a bundle, a supported edition, or the creator's own commercial skill. Asking someone to list their free MIT skill at £19 will not land. |
| **B. Register as the seller's own shop front** | Doug curates and sells bundles of his own skills; the register also lists other people's free skills for credibility and breadth | Lowest risk, no dependence on other creators converting, and matches the two paid entries that already exist. But it is a product business, not a marketplace. |
| **C. Listing fee / sponsorship** | Creators pay to be in the register | Directly contradicts the "no commission" promise being made in the outreach, and there is no audience yet to justify a fee. |

**Recommendation: run A and B together, and be honest about which is which.**

- The register's *catalogue* stays free-to-list and free-to-browse. Other people's skills are listed
  at £0, linking to source — this is the credibility layer and the SEO surface.
- The *paid* entries are things that genuinely are not free: Doug's own bundles, and any creator who
  has something productised to sell.
- Outreach to creators should therefore **not** pitch "list your free skill for £19". It should pitch
  two separate, honest offers:
  1. **List your skills for free** — a verified, curated entry linking to your repo, and
  2. **If you have a paid tier, a bundle, or support you want to sell** — the register has a
     checkout and takes no commission.

That is a smaller, slower, more honest business than "a marketplace", and it is one that the
evidence actually supports.

## What must change in the product

1. **The submission form needs a "free" path that is the default**, not an afterthought. Right now
   it asks for a price and a PayPal link with a "0.00 publishes it as a free entry" hint buried
   underneath. Free should be the obvious first option.
2. **The entries need a "what you get" line** that distinguishes a free download from a paid,
   supported product — a bundle, updates, support. Otherwise the price is arbitrary.
3. **The paid entries need to be Doug's own productised bundles** to start with. Two exist
   (Code Review £29, Prototype £19). A bundle — say "The Delivery Pack: TDD + code review +
   debugging + planning" — is a far better first product than any individual skill.
4. **The verification promise is the only real differentiator** and it is the one thing free
   directories do not do. It should be stated more strongly, and the testing should be visible:
   what was run, against what, and when.

## Open decisions for Doug

1. **Is the paid offer Doug's own bundles first, with other people's skills listed free?** (the
   recommendation) — or a pure third-party marketplace, accepting a slower start and a weaker
   first pitch?
2. **Should the register take a listing fee from creators who want a paid listing?** It is currently
   promised as "no commission" in every template. Charging contradicts the pitch and the budget
   argues against it. Recommendation: no.
3. **Who writes the first bundle?** The three highest-value free skills already identified in the
   catalogue are TDD, code review, and systematic debugging. That is a bundle already.
